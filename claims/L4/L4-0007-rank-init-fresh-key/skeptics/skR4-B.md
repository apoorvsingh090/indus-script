## Why the top-10 metric cannot depend on the key (structural result)

Under the prereg construction the encipher map is a bijection K: the cipher
label of plain type X is K[X], and labels occur in the enciphered half exactly
where their preimages occur. Hence for the half-2 stream:

- the ordered cipher-label ranking (Counter.most_common, ties by first stream
  occurrence) is the same ordering one gets from ranking half-2's plain types,
  relabelled through K — including tie order: **K-invariant**;
- the decode rule pairs cipher rank j with plain rank j (`ref_rank[j]` from
  half-1, itself a K-independent Counter over the reference stream);
- a present-type row hits iff its preimage has the same rank in half-1 and
  half-2 — independent of the drawn permutation;
- absent-type rows (0-count in half-2) can never enter `most_common(10)`.

So the top-10 hit count is a function of the corpus stream alone. Verified
empirically: every key ever drawn on this stream yields 5/10. The full-alphabet
count retains a small key-dependence only through absent labels' preimages
(`inv[c]`), which is why full spread is 8-10 across draws.

## Threshold semantics

- The bar's "top-10" set is half-2's ten most frequent types (Counter over the
  enciphered half, most_common(10)) — not an alphabetical or label-dependent
  notion; the ordered set is K-invariant under the prereg tie-break.
- Live exact tie in this stream: half-2 ranks 3/4, A 160 = MU 160. Prereg
  tie-break (stream first-occurrence) puts A at rank 3 — A@3 decodes
  ref_rank[3] = A → hit; MU@4 decodes LUGAL → miss. A label-coordinate
  tie-break instead makes the pairing draw-dependent: top10 flips 4/5 (24-draw
  sweep: 9 draws at 4, 15 at 5), still far under the bar.
- Fragile half boundaries (near-tie counts) worth noting as ordinary tie
  fragility: half-1 ranks 10/11 = KID 85 / DA 84 (boundary situation also seen
  in L4-0002's README), half-2 ranks 10/11 = GA 92 / |SAL.TUG2| 85; half-1
  ranks 8/9 = GA 91 = KA 91 (stream-order tie).
- If the bar were (mis)read against the full-alphabet count, the "7/10" text
  would be comparing 8-13/370 against 7-of-10 — category error; that is
  exactly the trap the L4-0003 artifact fell into (see Wording honesty).

## Wording honesty (objection)

- The title/commit-message framing ("the post-hoc 8/10 … was key-draw luck")
  and the README Reading bullet ("key-draw dependent … the '8/10' was one
  lucky draw inside a 5-8/10 band") are **refuted**: the top-10 hit count is
  mathematically key-invariant under the shipped algorithm and empirically
  constant (5/10 for L4-0002's key, L4-0003's key, the sealed key, and 0/24
  fresh draws reaching 7; even the most tie-sensitive variant tops out at 5).
  There is no 5-8/10 key-draw band.
- Where the "8/10" really comes from: in the L4-0003 run
  (`how-we-built-it/run_keyrec.py`), the rank-init branch computes
  `init_hits = sum(1 for i, c in enumerate(vocab) …)` — a **full-alphabet**
  count over all 370 positions — and stores it as `"rank_init_top10"`. The
  same code separates the hill-climb branch correctly
  (`hill_top10_hits` / `hill_full_hits`). On this identical stream that
  computation gives 8 on key2 = 8 on key3 (and 8 is the modal value across 24
  fresh draws: {8 x22, 9, 10}). So the "post-hoc 8/10" is actually **8/370
  (~2.2%), mislabeled as a top-10 count** — not a lucky top-10 draw.
- Recommended corrections (README title/Reading/table + future commit
  messages; chain history itself is immutable): rename L4-0003's stored field
  reading to "rank_init full-alphabet 8/370 (mislabeled as top-10 in its
  results.json)" and drop the "key-draw luck / 5-8 band" attribution in favour
  of the structural statement above.
